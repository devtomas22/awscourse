Domain 1: Cloud Concepts · 24% of scored content

Answered 0 of 24 · First-try correct: 0

1. A company's books show millions spent buying servers that sat mostly idle between seasonal peaks. Moving to AWS, which advantage of cloud computing addresses this directly?

Trading capital expense (buying and housing servers) for variable expense is the first cloud advantage — you pay as you consume, with no upfront commitment to peak-sized hardware.

2. Per-unit prices on AWS are consistently lower than a single company could achieve with its own hardware pool. Which mechanism produces this pricing?

Economies of scale: because hundreds of thousands of customers aggregate their usage on AWS, the scale is passed back as lower ongoing pay-as-you-go prices for everyone.

3. A product team in Singapore prepares a launch for users across Asia, Europe, and the Americas. Which advantage does standing up infrastructure in three Regions with a few clicks illustrate?

Go global in minutes: with a few clicks you deploy into AWS Regions worldwide, placing infrastructure near users wherever the product grows.

4. After a migration, the infrastructure team stops planning server-room capacity and instead ships product features. Which benefit of cloud computing is at work?

Stop spending money running and maintaining data centers: engineering effort moves from racking and stacking servers to serving customers and the product.

5. A team wants to test five proof-of-concept architectures this month and shut the failing ones down within days. Which benefit makes that practical?

Increased speed and agility: resources are available in minutes instead of weeks, so frequent, cheap, reversible experimentation becomes the norm.

6. A bank's risk team requires compute capacity split across multiple physically isolated facilities inside one geographic area. How does AWS model that requirement?

A Region subdivides into Availability Zones — each AZ is one or more discrete data centers, isolated from other AZs' failures yet interconnected with low-latency links.

7. Traffic keeps flowing while one Availability Zone undergoes a routine power failure. Which design property makes that possible?

Availability Zones are engineered for fault isolation — a storm or power event in one AZ does not affect the others, and high-bandwidth private links keep synchronized copies reachable.

8. A streaming platform wants its video served from cache sites near viewers worldwide instead of always from origin servers. Which AWS building block supplies those cache sites?

Edge locations are the cache sites CloudFront uses to deliver content close to viewers — they are not Regions, AZs, or general-purpose storage.

9. A workload's data may never leave Germany under the governing contract. Which factor decides the target Region?

Four factors drive Region choice — latency, price, service availability, and compliance. Data-residency law makes compliance the decisive one here.

10. A consultant lists candidate criteria for picking a workload's Region. Which one is NOT a legitimate selection factor?

Region selection weighs latency, price, service availability, and compliance — account-level IAM user counts say nothing about geography.

11. A two-tier application must survive the loss of one data-center building while remaining inside a single Region. Where do its instances run?

Multi-AZ deployment is the single-Region answer: each AZ carries independent power, cooling, and networking, so losing one building does not take the tier down.

12. Which Well-Architected pillar covers measuring the environmental impact of workloads and reducing the resources wasted in running them?

Sustainability (the sixth pillar, added in 2021) targets the energy and resource footprint of cloud workloads — related to, but distinct from, pure cost work.

13. A team's weekly review deletes unattached EBS volumes and right-sizes over-provisioned instances. Which pillar does that discipline serve?

Cost Optimization means running the business at the lowest price point — right-sizing, stopping idle resources, and matching spend to actual demand.

14. Releases ship as small reversible changes, each failure gets a review, and runbooks improve after every incident. Which pillar is the team practicing?

Operational Excellence: run and monitor systems, keep changes frequent and reversible, and continually refine procedures from incidents.

15. A workload must recover automatically from component failure and keep meeting demand that triples every December. Which pillar sets these requirements?

Reliability is the ability to recover from failures and satisfy demand — automatic recovery, horizontal scaling, and tested recovery procedures.

16. A team replaces a hand-rolled VM fleet with serverless functions and containers sized to actual load. Which pillar does this efficiency work belong to?

Performance Efficiency: use computing resources efficiently and adopt best-fit technologies — serverless and containers — as requirements evolve.

17. Every Friday evening an application's traffic quadruples, then returns to baseline overnight. Which approach serves that profile most cost-effectively?

Elasticity: acquire and release resources as demand changes so you pay only for capacity actually serving traffic — no idle peak fleet, no fixed reservations.

18. A company historically bought peak-season servers that idled 350 days a year before migrating. Which principle did the migration apply?

Stop guessing capacity: cloud access lets you provision from real demand and scale as needs change, instead of pre-buying peak hardware that sits idle.

19. Before signing a migration plan, finance wants a month-by-month estimate of the PROPOSED AWS architecture. Which tool produces that?

The Pricing Calculator models a not-yet-existing configuration by service and Region into a monthly estimate, exportable as CSV — Cost Explorer and Budgets analyze spending that already happened.

20. A platform team adopted Amazon RDS instead of self-administering MySQL on EC2. What did they actually eliminate?

Managed services remove undifferentiated heavy lifting: with RDS, AWS operates the engine and its OS, while the team keeps schema design, tuning, and the data.

21. How does running on AWS strengthen a small company's security posture?

Increased security posture at scale: AWS aggregates knowledge of threats seen across literally everyone else's workloads into automated, continuously updated defenses.

22. A retailer must serve European users with low latency AND keep European data inside Europe. Which choice satisfies both goals?

Latency and compliance are both first-class Region-selection factors — a European Region serves both; edge locations cache content but are not data-residency storage.

23. Which sentence pairs each cloud benefit with its actual mechanism?

Two advantages rest on aggregation: economies of scale come from aggregated usage feeding lower prices, and increased security posture comes from aggregated threat intelligence feeding automated processes.

24. Which statement best describes why the Well-Architected Framework exists?

The framework's six pillars — operational excellence, security, reliability, performance efficiency, cost optimization, sustainability — give teams one consistent vocabulary for weighing trade-offs over a workload's lifetime.

Domain 2: Security and Compliance · 30% of scored content

Answered 0 of 30 · First-try correct: 0

25. A three-tier application runs managed PostgreSQL on RDS alongside self-managed application instances on EC2. Who patches which layer?

The split shifts with the service: EC2 is infrastructure (customer patches the guest OS), RDS is managed (AWS patches the engine and OS) — but customer-side duties never disappear.

26. Which duty NEVER transfers to AWS, regardless of which service the company runs?

Across every service model, data classification, protection choices, and identity management stay with the customer — AWS never owns the content or its handling rules.

27. An auditor asks which routine task still requires the account root user even after workday sign-ins are federated. Which is it?

Root-only tasks include closing the account and changing account email or contact information — daily work belongs on IAM identities, federated or not.

28. Which devices qualify as registered MFA factors in an AWS account?

MFA is a second factor on sign-in — a hardware device or a virtual TOTP app — recommended above all on the root user; the other options are unrelated control types.

29. A developer needs read access to exactly one S3 bucket. Which IAM policy best expresses least privilege?

Least privilege: grant the minimum actions on the minimum resources and add only when a real requirement appears — never broad wildcards or shared admin.

30. Hundreds of warehouse devices push telemetry to AWS around the clock. Which credential design avoids embedding long-lived keys in the devices?

Roles issue temporary credentials through STS: nothing long-lived is stored, credentials expire on their own, and revocation is instant — the default for workloads.

31. Why does an IAM administrator prefer groups over editing user policies one by one?

Attach the policy to the group once; membership carries the permissions — administration scales without per-user edits.

32. An identity policy explicitly allows an action while another attached policy explicitly denies the same action. What is the outcome?

Evaluation ends at an explicit deny: no allow, however it is attached, can grant an action that another policy explicitly denies.

33. A freshly created IAM user with no policies attempts to list the account's buckets. What happens?

Implicit deny is the default: each request starts denied and stays denied until some policy grants it — and an explicit deny would then hard-block it.

34. Which settings does an account-level password policy actually control?

The password policy is account-wide for IAM user passwords — length, complexity, rotation. It has nothing to do with encryption, MFA hardware, or networking.

35. Employees should sign in once with corporate directory credentials and reach several AWS accounts. Which service was built for this?

IAM Identity Center (formerly AWS Single Sign-On) federates workforce identity from the corporate directory and assigns permissions across accounts from one sign-in.

36. A consumer app with millions of end customers needs its own sign-up and sign-in. Which service fits — and why not the one used for workforce SSO?

Cognito handles customer-facing sign-up, sign-in, and federation for apps; Identity Center federates employees into AWS accounts — two different populations entirely.

37. Which statement about IAM roles is correct?

A role is a set of permissions a principal assumes via STS for a session — cross-account access, compute workloads, federation — never long-lived secrets.

38. Data crossing the internet from a user's browser to an AWS service endpoint must resist interception. Which control is present at every service endpoint?

Encryption in transit: every AWS service endpoint speaks HTTPS over TLS, so credentials and payloads never traverse the internet in the clear.

39. An attacker obtains a raw volume snapshot file outside any AWS account. Which control makes the contained data unreadable to them?

Encryption at rest turns stored data into ciphertext on the medium — a stolen snapshot or disk is worthless without the key; MFA and routing controls protect access paths, not media.

40. Nobody touches the encryption settings as an object lands in a bucket. What state is the object in?

S3 encrypts every new object by default with server-side encryption using SSE-S3 managed keys, at no extra cost — bucket settings only change which key type is used.

41. What does AWS KMS actually do for an application?

KMS creates, stores, and controls the use of encryption keys — envelope encryption, key policies, and a CloudTrail record of key usage; certificates and malware scanning belong to other services.

42. A customer-managed symmetric KMS key sits untouched since creation. What does automatic rotation do?

Automatic rotation is on by default for customer-managed symmetric keys at 365 days — the key ID is stable while the backing material cycles; asymmetric keys are not auto-rotated.

43. An investigator must prove who deleted a production bucket, and from which IP address, two months ago. Which service holds that evidence?

CloudTrail is the API audit log: action, identity, source IP, and timestamp. Config tracks configuration — it does not answer who did what from where.

44. A developer opens the CloudTrail console today and queries Event history without ever creating a trail. How deep can they look back?

Event history shows the last 90 days of management events; a durable, searchable record beyond that — or data events at all — requires a configured trail.

45. Which pairing of service to purpose is right?

CloudTrail is the action log auditing API activity; Config is the configuration log tracking resource states over time and evaluating them against rules — the exam's favorite distinction.

46. Threat hunters want machine-learning detectors fed by CloudTrail, VPC flow logs, and DNS logs to flag compromised credentials. Which service?

GuardDuty ingests CloudTrail management events, VPC flow logs, and DNS logs, applies ML, and raises findings on anomalies such as credential misuse.

47. Patching teams want continuous CVE scanning across EC2 instances and container images in ECR. Which service does that?

Inspector is automated vulnerability management — continuous scanning of EC2, ECR container images, and Lambda functions for known CVEs and exposure.

48. Compliance knows sensitive customer records sit somewhere among thousands of S3 objects. Which service locates the PII?

Macie discovers and reports sensitive data — especially PII — inside Amazon S3; the others watch configuration, vulnerabilities, or access behavior, not object content.

49. A CISO wants GuardDuty, Inspector, and Macie findings consolidated on one pane and scored against security standards. Which service assembles that?

Security Hub aggregates security findings across services and accounts and evaluates them against standards such as CIS AWS Foundations.

50. An auditor requests AWS's PCI compliance documentation and a signed agreement. Which self-service route delivers both?

Artifact is the self-service portal for compliance reports and agreement review and acceptance — evidence is downloaded directly by the customer.

51. Which layer-7 control blocks injection-pattern web requests in front of an Application Load Balancer?

WAF inspects web request content at layer 7 against rules — including AWS managed rule groups — on web ACLs attached to CloudFront, ALB, or API Gateway; NACLs see only ports and addresses.

52. Which protection runs automatically for every AWS customer at no additional charge?

Shield Standard protects all customers against common layers 3 and 4 DDoS automatically and free; Shield Advanced is the paid tier adding response support for critical workloads.

53. Where does a database password that must rotate itself every 30 days belong?

Secrets Manager stores AND rotates secrets on a schedule — Parameter Store SecureString is the lighter static store without built-in rotation; scripts store secrets far too loosely.

54. A company operates under GDPR in Europe and healthcare privacy rules in the US. How should it plan compliance mapping on AWS?

Compliance obligations differ by geography and industry — AWS compliance resources plus Artifact program agreements let obligations be mapped regime by regime.

Domain 3: Cloud Technology and Services · 34% of scored content

Answered 0 of 34 · First-try correct: 0

55. Traffic to a web fleet must spread across instances in two AZs, and the fleet must grow and shrink with demand. Which pairing handles both?

ELB spreads incoming requests across registered targets in every AZ while Auto Scaling adjusts the fleet to demand — the standard elastic pair.

56. An API tier routes /orders to one service and /users to another behind a single load balancer. Which load balancer type?

ALB operates at layer 7 and routes by request attributes — path, host, headers — across target groups; NLB sees only layer-4 flows and cannot route on content.

57. A market-data feed must sustain extreme TCP throughput with very low latency. Which load balancer fits the layer-4 profile?

NLB operates at layer 4 for very high-throughput TCP/UDP at extreme performance; ALB is the HTTP router, and the others serve different roles entirely.

58. A nightly export needs 40 minutes of compute per run with no server for the team to manage. Which option fits?

Lambda's hard cap is 15 minutes per invocation, so the 40-minute job cannot run there; Fargate executes the container with no server or fleet to operate.

59. An operations team schedules Docker container tasks on a fleet of EC2 instances whose capacity they control themselves. Which orchestration service?

ECS schedules containers on either a customer-managed EC2 fleet (the team sizes and patches it) or serverless Fargate — owning the instances means the EC2 launch type.

60. Which option lets a team run containers while AWS operates BOTH the container instances and the underlying servers?

Fargate is the serverless engine for ECS and EKS: declare CPU and memory per task and AWS runs both the container and its host — no fleet, no nodes.

61. A platform team must run certified Kubernetes with AWS managing the control plane while they keep operating the worker nodes. Which service?

EKS runs upstream Kubernetes — AWS operates the HA control plane across AZs; data-plane worker nodes (or Fargate) remain the team's responsibility.

62. A startup deploys a Python web app and wants capacity, load balancing, and health monitoring arranged automatically — without losing access to the underlying resources. Which service?

Beanstalk provisions the surrounding platform — EC2, ELB, Auto Scaling, health monitoring — from your code and configuration, while every underlying resource stays reachable for tuning.

63. A manufacturer must run AWS API-compatible compute physically inside its factory, where data cannot leave and round-trip latency must be near zero. Which offering?

Outposts delivers AWS compute and storage into the customer's own facility with local processing — built for data-residency and low-latency on-premises work.

64. A compliance archive is written once, read at most once a year, and retrieval time of hours is acceptable. Which storage class minimizes cost?

Archive classes trade retrieval time and per-GB retrieval fees for the lowest storage price — Deep Archive is the cheapest tier for data almost never touched.

65. Objects drop out of active use after two months. What moves them to a cheaper class automatically, with no operator involvement?

Lifecycle rules automate transitions and expirations — moving objects between storage classes by age or prefix without anyone touching the bucket.

66. An auditor challenges the numbers behind S3 Standard's reliability reputation. What is the commonly stated design target for object DURABILITY?

S3 Standard is designed for eleven nines of durability by redundantly storing objects across multiple facilities — durability is per-object and annual, distinct from availability.

67. A database on EC2 needs a 2 TB block device with low latency and snapshots into S3, attachable to exactly one instance in one AZ. Which storage?

EBS is network-attached block storage bound to one instance within one AZ, with snapshots copying to S3 — EFS mounts everywhere, S3 is object storage, instance store is ephemeral.

68. A rendering box needs scratch space for terabytes per day, wiped when the instance stops or fails. Which storage matches that lifecycle?

Instance store is local to the host: fast for buffers, caches, and scratch data, but data is lost on stop, hibernation, or failure — the opposite of EBS and EFS persistence.

69. Hundreds of Linux instances across three AZs share one POSIX file system whose capacity grows automatically with usage. Which service?

EFS provides elastic NFS storage — thousands of instances mount it across AZs, and it grows and shrinks with the data set; EBS is single-instance and block-based.

70. A Windows application cluster needs a managed file share presenting NTFS semantics over SMB. Which service?

FSx provides fully managed third-party file systems — Windows/SMB with NTFS ACLs, or Lustre for HPC — natively integrated with AWS services.

71. A hospital must move 800 TB of archives off-site with no usable internet uplink this quarter. Which AWS mechanism fits?

The Snow family moves bulk data physically — a Snowball Edge device carries up to petabytes, bypassing the internet entirely; the network options either use the internet or wait on provisioning.

72. Instances in a private subnet must download OS updates from the internet while rejecting every unsolicited inbound connection. Which component, placed where?

The NAT gateway lives in a PUBLIC subnet (which routes through an internet gateway) and provides one-way egress for private subnets — private-side placement is the classic wrong answer.

73. Traffic replies to connections that a security group allowed inbound keep flowing. Why?

Statefulness: an SG tracks established connections, so replies need no extra outbound rule — a stateless NACL would need matching entries in both directions.

74. Which combination of statements about Network ACLs is accurate?

NACLs are subnet-scoped and stateless — return traffic must be explicitly allowed — with numbered rules applied from lowest to highest, first match wins.

75. An enterprise needs a dedicated private link between its data center and AWS with consistent latency, never touching the public internet. Which service?

Direct Connect is a dedicated physical circuit into AWS — private, predictable latency; VPN tunnels ride the public internet instead.

76. The fastest to stand up is an encrypted channel between an office network and a VPC, over the internet. Which service?

Site-to-Site VPN establishes encrypted IPsec tunnels in minutes to hours — Direct Connect is the slower dedicated-line alternative for when it is finally built.

77. Beyond resolving DNS names, what can Amazon Route 53 do for a fleet?

Route 53 is DNS plus domain registration plus health-check-driven failover routing — resilience applied at the name-resolution layer.

78. Fifty VPCs plus on-premises sites all need to reach one another; a peer-to-peer mesh is unmanageable. Which component centralizes the routing?

Transit Gateway is the hub: attach VPCs, VPNs, and Direct Connect connections once and route centrally — no peer mesh, no per-pair management.

79. VPC A peers with B, and B peers with C. A sends traffic destined for C, expecting B to relay it. What happens?

A peering connects exactly two VPCs; traffic does not transit through a chain — A needs its own peering to C, or a Transit Gateway instead.

80. A finance application needs a managed relational engine, automated backups, and a warm standby in a second AZ. Which service?

RDS runs managed relational engines with automated backup, patching, and a Multi-AZ standby that takes over on failure — DynamoDB and ElastiCache are different database families.

81. A MySQL-compatible workload needs faster replication and up to 15 read replicas. Which managed database fits?

Aurora is MySQL- and PostgreSQL-compatible on a distributed storage layer — up to 15 Aurora Replicas with much f1|aster failover than standard RDS replication.

82. A multiplayer game needs single-digit-millisecond reads and writes on key-value data that scales without capacity planning. Which database?

DynamoDB is a serverless NoSQL key-value database delivering single-digit-millisecond performance at any scale — built for high-velocity game data.

83. An analyst team queries petabytes of historical data for dashboards and BI reports. Which purpose-built service?

Redshift is the petabyte-scale data warehouse using columnar storage and parallel query for OLAP and BI — transactional stores like RDS or DynamoDB are the wrong shape for this.

84. Two microservices must exchange work items without blocking on each other while consumers are temporarily down. Which pattern?

SQS queues decouple producers from consumers by storing messages until they are processed — Standard queues are at-least-once, and FIFO adds strict ordering.

85. An order pipeline MUST process each item exactly once, in strict sequence. Which queue type?

FIFO queues guarantee exactly-once processing and strict ordering; Standard queues are at-least-once with best-effort ordering — neither SNS nor an archive replay substitutes.

86. One publishing event must reach many subscribers at once — queues, Lambda functions, email, and mobile push. Which service?

SNS fans a published message out to every subscribed endpoint simultaneously — SQS queues, Lambda, HTTP, email, SMS, and mobile push.

87. What does Amazon EventBridge add that plain point-to-point messaging does not?

EventBridge is an event bus: rules match event patterns and route them to targets, and Scheduler delivers recurring jobs at cron-like times — routing, not raw fan-out or delivery guarantees.

88. A refund workflow needs retries with backoff, choice branches, and parallel approval steps, with state visible between stages. Which service orchestrates it?

Step Functions coordinates workflows as state machines — task retries, branching, parallel execution, full state visibility — beyond what queues or event rules coordinate.

Domain 4: Billing, Pricing, and Support · 12% of scored content

89. Which sentence states the core AWS pricing principle most accurately?

Pay-as-you-go: pay for what you use, when you use it, with no long-term contract — the foundation every other pricing model bends around.

90. A fault-tolerant batch job tolerates interruption and should cost as little as possible. Which purchasing option fits?

Spot prices spare EC2 capacity at up to 90% below On-Demand and reclaims it on two minutes' notice — made for interruptible, checkpointed work.

91. Steady-state production has run the same instance type continuously for months and will for the next three years. Which option cuts this cost the most?

Reserved Instances commit to a 1- or 3-year term with all, partial, or no upfront payment for up to 72% off — the deepest sustained discount; Spot cannot hold a steady state.

92. A team's monthly usage shifts across instance families, sizes, and operating systems. What does a Savings Plans commitment actually lock in?

Savings Plans commit a consistent spend (for example 10 USD per hour) for 1 or 3 years; the discount then follows eligible usage wherever it lands — more flexible than reserving instance types.

93. For which direction does AWS data transfer generate internet charges?

The rulebook: data IN to AWS is free, data OUT to the internet is billed at class rates (with the first 100 GB per month free on internet egress per current docs) — always check the direction before pricing a design.

94. Which tool estimates the monthly cost of an architecture that does not exist yet?

The Pricing Calculator models a proposed configuration into a monthly estimate before anything exists — Cost Explorer, Budgets, and the CUR all analyze spending that already occurred.

95. Creating a My Monthly Cost Budget template on a 10 USD budget — which alerts does it wire up?

The template wires three alerts — actual 85%, actual 100%, and forecast 100% — and remember that alert email recipients must be verified before alerts fire.

96. Which tool charts 13 months of actual spend and forecasts up to 12 months ahead, sliceable by service and tag?

Cost Explorer visualizes usage and cost with 13 months of history and up to 12 months of forecast, grouped by service, account, tag, and more; Budgets only alerts, and the Calculator only estimates.

97. Finance must load the most granular usage-and-cost record into Athena for SQL analysis. Which deliverable?

The CUR delivers the most detailed usage and cost data as zipped files to S3, with report splitting for large datasets — query it directly with Athena.

98. Under consolidated billing in AWS Organizations, what happens to volume discounts and Reserved Instance benefits?

Consolidated billing pools usage for volume tiering and shares Reserved Instance and Savings Plan discounts across member accounts — while each member still sees its own costs.

99. Which Support plan pairs a designated Technical Account Manager with 15-minute response SLAs?

Enterprise Support adds the TAM, Concierge billing, and 15-minute SLAs; the required minimum dropped from 15,000 to 5,000 USD under the current plan lineup — Business has no TAM.
