Summary

  • Cost isn’t automatic. Moving to the cloud doesn’t make things cheaper by itself — you pay for what you use, so over-provisioned capacity and forgotten resources are billed all the same. The win is trading fixed expense (CAPEX) for variable (OPEX), guessing capacity less, and tapping the same economies of scale as everyone else — with TCO thinking and the Pricing Calculator to stay honest.
  • Compute purchasing models: On-Demand (pay by the second/hour, no commitment), Reserved Instances (up to 72% off for 1–3-year commitments with all/partial/no upfront payment), Savings Plans (commit to dollars-per-hour of usage instead of instance types), and Spot (up to 90% off, interruptible with a two-minute warning — batch and flexible jobs first).
  • Spend-lean compute habits: right-size instances, Auto Scaling to match demand, switch dev/test environments off at nights and weekends, and let Compute Optimizer suggest right-sized types from ML on your usage.
  • Storage cost levers: the price per GB varies by S3 storage class (Standard vs Infrequent Access vs the Glacier tiers), lifecycle policies move objects for you (transition to IA, expire stale versions, abort incomplete multipart uploads), and archive classes charge per-GB retrieval when you read data back.
  • Data transfer rulebook: inbound is free, outbound is billed — about ten cents per GB, with the first 100 GB/month per account free per today’s docs; cross-AZ and cross-region data movement adds charges on top, and CloudFront egress is cheaper than serving straight out of EC2/S3.
  • Monitor and predict: AWS Budgets alerts on actual and forecasted spend (email or SNS), Cost Explorer charts 13 months of history and forecasts 12 months out, and the Cost and Usage Report is the most detailed view — delivered once a day to S3 in zipped files split at up to 500 MB and queried with Athena.
  • Budget demo numbers: the My Monthly Cost Budget template on a $10 budget created three default alerts — actual spend at 85% ($8.50), actual at 100%, and forecasted 100% — and recipients must verify their email before anything fires.
  • Multi-account billing: consolidated billing in AWS Organizations puts every member account on the payer’s bill with pooled volume discounts and Reserved Instance/Savings Plan sharing, member accounts still see and track their own costs, and Billing Conductor stacks custom billing views for resellers.
  • Tags make costs legible: cost allocation tags must be activated in the Billing console before Cost Explorer or the CUR can group with them; resource groups organize tagged resources; and Trusted Advisor cost checks flag idle load balancers and unattached EBS volumes (the seven core checks are included on Basic support per today’s docs).
  • Support, 2026 edition: the course teaches Developer $29 / Business $100 / Enterprise On-Ramp $5,500 / Enterprise $15,000 — today’s docs discontinue Developer, Business, and Enterprise On-Ramp on January 1, 2027: the current lineup is Basic (free), Business Support+ from $29/month with AI-powered 24/7 help, Enterprise Support with a designated TAM, 15-minute responses and a minimum cut to $5,000 (was $15,000), plus AWS Unified Operations.
  • Beyond tickets: re:Post (the official AWS community), Knowledge Center articles, the AWS Partner Network, Managed Services for 24/7 operations, and Activate credits for startups — then the exam framing: billing, pricing, and support is the smallest CLF-C02 domain at 12% of scored content.

Main concepts

Pay-as-you-go & the shared cost responsibility
You pay only for what you use — variable expense instead of fixed capacity — but nothing gets cheaper automatically: every idle instance and forgotten snapshot is billed. AWS sets the prices; optimizing usage is your half of the deal. Course context: billing, pricing, and support is the CLF-C02’s smallest domain at 12% of scored content (docs-verified in the exam guide).
TCO and pricing-model trade-offs
Compare On-Demand flexibility, Reserved/Savings-Plan commitments, and Spot interruptions before you launch; use the AWS Pricing Calculator to estimate a monthly bill and export it as CSV. Course example framing: fixed expense → variable expense, stop guessing capacity, economies of scale.
Compute purchasing options (the discount ladder)
On-Demand: no commitment, pay per second or hour. Reserved Instances: up to 72% off for 1- or 3-year terms with all-upfront, partial-upfront, or no-upfront payment. Savings Plans: commit to a dollars-per-hour spend over 1 or 3 years and the discount follows across eligible usage. Spot: up to 90% off on-demand prices, reclaimable on two minutes’ notice — designed for interruptible, fault-tolerant, or very flexible workloads.
Spend-lean compute operations
Right-size to the workload, use Auto Scaling so capacity tracks demand, park dev/test environments at nights and weekends, and run Compute Optimizer — its ML recommendations suggest smaller or better-fitting instance types from observed utilization.
Storage classes and lifecycle automation
S3 storage classes price per GB differently (Standard is the frequent-access benchmark; Infrequent Access trades cheaper storage for per-GB retrieval fees; the Glacier tiers are coldest and cheapest). Lifecycle policies automate the economics: transition objects to IA after a day count, expire stale versions, and abort incomplete multipart uploads that would otherwise sit billed.
Data transfer: the quiet bill
Data transfer IN is free. Outbound to the internet is billed — about ten cents per GB, with the first 100 GB/month per account free (today’s docs). Cross-AZ and cross-region movement add their own charges, so replication has a bill too. Serving content via CloudFront prices egress lower than serving it straight out of EC2/S3 — a cost lever as much as a performance one.
The cost-monitoring toolset
Budgets watches actual & forecasted spend against a budget and alerts by email or SNS; Cost Explorer visualizes up to 13 months of history and forecasts up to 12 months ahead; the Cost and Usage Report is the most granular record — delivered to S3 once a day, split into zipped files of up to 500 MB, ready for Athena queries.
Budget demo: $10 and the three default alerts
Billing console → Budgets → Create budget → template My Monthly Cost Budget → name it, set $10, add a recipient email, create. The template wires three default alerts: 85% of actual ($8.50), 100% of actual, and 100% of forecasted; recipients get a verification email and nothing fires until it’s confirmed. Delete the budget to stop alerts.
Multi-account billing with Organizations
Consolidated billing: one payer account receives every member account’s charges on a single bill; aggregate usage unlocks pooled volume discounts, and Reserved Instances/Savings Plans can be shared across member accounts. Members keep visibility of their own costs. Billing Conductor adds custom billing views (reseller-style showback) without moving any data.
Tags, groups, and Trusted Advisor’s cost eye
Tag resources by project/environment, activate cost allocation tags in the Billing console, then group with resource tags. Trusted Advisor cost checks surface savings: idle load balancers, unattached EBS volumes, under-used instances — today’s docs: check-level access on Basic support is seven core checks.
Support plans — course picture vs 2026 docs
Course tiers: Basic (free), Developer $29/mo, Business from $100/mo, Enterprise On-Ramp $5,500/mo, Enterprise $15,000/mo with a dedicated TAM. Update from today’s docs: Developer, Business, and Enterprise On-Ramp are discontinued January 1, 2027 (GovCloud keeps the legacy plans). The current lineup: Basic (free 24/7 billing/quota help), Business Support+ from $29/month with AI-powered assistance and 24/7 Cloud Support Engineers, Enterprise Support (designated TAM, 15-minute responses, Security Incident Response included) at a $5,000 minimum, reduced from $15,000, and AWS Unified Operations.
Other sources of support
Self-serve first: docs, whitepapers, Knowledge Center KB articles, re:Post (official AWS community). Then people: AWS Partner Network consultants, Managed Services for 24/7 operations, AWS IQ-style expert help, and Activate credits for startups. Support tickets flow through the Support Center console.

Quizzes

Pop quiz covers the module; real-world picks map situations to the right tool; the flow drill picks the cost tool for the job — first try counts.

1. Compare On-Demand, Reserved Instances, Savings Plans, and Spot — when does each win?

Show answer
On-Demand: unpredictable or short-lived needs, no commitment. Reserved Instances: steady-state usage you can commit to for 1–3 years — up to 72% off. Savings Plans: same commitment idea but you commit to dollars-per-hour of usage, not instance types. Spot: interruptible, fault-tolerant work — up to 90% off with a two-minute reclaim warning.

2. Why is moving to the cloud not automatically cheaper, and what changes the math?

Show answer
You pay for what you use — so waste is billed too. The savings come from the model: variable instead of fixed expense, no capacity guessing, shared economies of scale — plus active optimization: right-sizing, Auto Scaling, parking dev/test environments, and cleaner storage/transfer choices.

3. Name the three default alerts the $10 budget demo creates, with the dollar figures.

Show answer
The My Monthly Cost Budget template wires three alerts on a $10 budget: actual spend at 85% = $8.50, actual spend at 100% = $10, and forecasted spend at 100%. Recipients must verify their email first; alerts stop if you delete the budget.

4. How does consolidated billing save money for an organization?

Show answer
One payer account receives every member account’s charges on a single bill. Aggregated usage unlocks pooled volume discounts, and Reserved Instances/Savings Plans can be shared across member accounts instead of being trapped in one account.

5. What does the course say about support tiers — and what changed in 2026 docs?

Show answer
Course: Basic free, Developer $29, Business from $100, Enterprise On-Ramp $5,500, Enterprise $15,000 with a dedicated TAM. Today’s docs: Developer, Business, and Enterprise On-Ramp are discontinued January 1, 2027; current plans are Basic (free), Business Support+ from $29/month, Enterprise Support with TAM + 15-minute responses at a $5,000 minimum (down from $15,000), and AWS Unified Operations.

6. How do S3 storage classes and lifecycle policies cut storage costs?

Show answer
Storage prices vary per class (Standard, Infrequent Access, Glacier tiers). Lifecycle policies automate the moves: transition objects to IA after a set day count, expire stale versions, abort incomplete multipart uploads. Archive classes trade the cheapest storage for per-GB retrieval when you read data back.

1. A lab workload runs only on weekday office hours.

Show answer
It shouldn’t run outside them: schedule start/stop so the stack is off at nights and weekends. Idle compute is the classic silent cost.

2. A data-processing job is resumable and can tolerate interruptions.

Show answer
Run it on Spot Instances: up to 90% off, reclaimed with a two-minute warning — perfect for interruptible, fault-tolerant work.

3. A steady production database host has run unchanged for months.

Show answer
Price it with a 1- or 3-year Reserved Instance: steady-state usage is exactly what commitments are for — up to 72% off.

4. Finance wants the bill split by project, but cost groups show nothing.

Show answer
Cost allocation tags must be activated in the Billing console first; only after activation do Cost Explorer and the CUR group by them.

5. A reseller owns 40 customer accounts and must show each one its own invoice picture.

Show answer
Billing Conductor: custom billing views over member accounts (showback/showbill) without moving underlying costs; the org itself rides consolidated billing with pooled discounts.

6. A startup wants credits and operational help, not enterprise phone support.

Show answer
AWS Activate for credits; help via re:Post and Knowledge Center; a Partner Network consultant when outside hands are needed — Business Support+ from $29/month when AI-assisted 24/7 support becomes worthwhile.
