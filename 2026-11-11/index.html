Day 2026-11-11 · FinOps — Cost Management: Identify & Remediate

Cost-management notes — the FinOps loop (Inform, Optimize, Operate) with ownership and FOCUS; the identification lineup — Cost Optimization Hub’s consolidated recommendations with quantified savings, Compute Optimizer’s opt-in rightsizing over a 14-day lookback, Cost Explorer’s default and Savings Plans utilization/coverage reports, and Cost Anomaly Detection’s managed monitors; then the remediation tactics — rightsizing and idle deletion, Instance Scheduler stop/start, Intelligent-Tiering and gp3 conversions, free gateway endpoints instead of NAT processing, and the Savings Plans/RI/Spot decision.

Day 14 · Nov 11, 2026Topic: FinOps & cost managementCLF-C02: Domain 4 · Billing, Pricing & Support (12%)Source: AWS documentation + finops.org · studied Nov 11, 2026

Summary

  • FinOps is a loop, not a dashboard (strategy). Inform = visibility and allocation (CUR, Cost Explorer, activated cost allocation tags, cost categories); Optimize = act on identified waste; Operate = governance with budgets, alerts, and recurring reviews. Principles: every team collaborates and takes ownership, and cloud spend is a variable cost model judged by business value (unit economics), with FOCUS as the open billing-data standard.
  • Identify first — the consolidated surface. Cost Optimization Hub pulls rightsizing, idle-resource deletion, and Savings Plans/RI purchase recommendations into one prioritized list across accounts and Regions, quantifies estimated monthly savings net of your existing commercial terms, and offers a cost efficiency metric for benchmarks and goals.
  • Identify second — the ML rightsizing engine. Compute Optimizer is opt-in, analyzes specs plus CloudWatch utilization for the last 14 days, and recommends for 14 resource types — EC2, Auto Scaling groups, EBS, Lambda, ECS on Fargate, commercial licenses, Aurora/RDS, NAT Gateways, DynamoDB, ElastiCache, MemoryDB, DocumentDB, WorkSpaces, SageMaker. The paid enhanced infrastructure metrics option extends the lookback to 93 days.
  • Identify third — the reporting window. Cost Explorer’s default reports can’t be modified — clone them into custom ones: Daily costs (last six months + forecast), monthly by service or linked account, EC2 running hours, Marketplace, and Reserved Instance reports. For commitments, the utilization report shows the share of your committed $ per hour you actually use; the coverage report shows the share of eligible usage still billed on-demand.
  • Identify fourth — the watchdog. Cost Anomaly Detection machine-learns your patterns instead of waiting for a threshold. Managed monitors auto-track AWS services, linked accounts, cost allocation tags, and cost categories — new accounts and tag values join automatically; custom monitors add your own thresholds. Alerts flow through SNS, chat applications, EventBridge, or AWS User Notifications; monitors for linked accounts/tags/categories are created only in the management account.
  • Remediate — compute. Rightsize from recommendations; delete idle resources; stop/start on schedules with Instance Scheduler on AWS (a tags + Lambda solution across Regions and accounts, handles EC2 and RDS, supports hibernation — a 50-of-168-hour fleet can save up to 70%); keep interruptible burst work on Spot, which discounts up to 90%.
  • Remediate — storage. Lifecycle policies for known patterns; S3 Intelligent-Tiering for unknown ones (no retrieval fees, small per-object monitoring charge, objects < 128 KB stay at Frequent rates with no fee); convert gp2 → gp3 online with Elastic Volumes — no detach or restart, no charge to modify, up to four modifications per rolling 24 hours, and you can later neither shrink nor cancel one.
  • Remediate — network. A gateway VPC endpoint for S3 and DynamoDB costs nothing extra and routes private-subnet traffic without an internet gateway or NAT device — while NAT gateways bill hourly plus per-GB data processing. Point S3/DynamoDB traffic at the endpoint first.
  • Remediate — commitments vs flexibility. Steady predictable load → Savings Plans (dollar-per-hour commitment, 1 or 3 years; a $10/hour example) or RIs; spiky unpredictable → on-demand; interruptible → Spot. Utilization < 100% means unused commitment; coverage < 100% means on-demand spend you may not have bargained for.
  • Govern and hold the line. Budgets enforce planned thresholds while anomaly detection catches learned-pattern deviations; repeat the Inform–Optimize–Operate cycle on a cadence — the exam rewards mapping each tool to its job (Domain 4 is 12% of scored content).

Main concepts

Four clusters — the FinOps model, the identification surfaces, the remediation tactics, and the commitment decision — each mapped to cost-tactic task statements.

The FinOps operating model: Inform, Optimize, Operate
Inform builds visibility: CUR as the line-item source, Cost Explorer for trends and forecasts, activated cost allocation tags and user-defined cost categories for allocation. Optimize attacks waste found by the identify surfaces. Operate keeps score: budgets, anomaly alerts, KPIs, and a recurring review cadence with engineering, finance, and business teams at one table. Ownership is a FinOps principle — teams own the cost of what they build.
FOCUS, unit economics, and the WA pillar
FOCUS (FinOps Open Cost and Usage Specification) standardizes billing data across vendors so tooling can compare apples to apples. Unit economics asks the value question — cost per customer, transaction, or model run — instead of raw spend. The Well-Architected cost-optimization pillar lists five practice areas: Practice Cloud Financial Management, Expenditure and usage awareness, Cost effective resources, Manage demand and supply resources, and Optimize over time.
Cost Optimization Hub in depth
A Billing and Cost Management feature that consolidates and prioritizes recommendations across your accounts and Regions: resource rightsizing, idle resource deletion, and Savings Plans and RI purchases (18+ recommendation types per the product page). It quantifies and aggregates estimated savings — discounting by your existing commercial terms so you buy only what adds savings — and gives a cost efficiency metric to benchmark accounts and set goals. Exam shortcut: one consolidated org-wide optimization view → Optimization Hub.
Compute Optimizer in depth
Opt in (standalone, member, or management account). It machine-learns over specifications and utilization metrics from CloudWatch for the last 14 days — vCPU, memory, network, disk — and produces rightsizing and idle-resource findings with savings estimates. Supported types: EC2 instances, Auto Scaling groups, EBS volumes, Lambda functions, ECS-on-Fargate services, commercial software licenses, Aurora/RDS databases, NAT Gateways, DynamoDB, ElastiCache, MemoryDB, DocumentDB, WorkSpaces, SageMaker. Activating enhanced infrastructure metrics (paid) stretches the lookback to 93 days for selected resources.
Cost Explorer: defaults, clones, and dimensions
Cost Explorer ships default reports you cannot modify — AWS Marketplace, Daily costs (~six months with forecast), Monthly costs by linked account, Monthly costs by service, Monthly EC2 running hours costs and usage, plus Reserved Instance reports (utilization among them) — but any default can seed a custom report. Slice by service, account, region, tag, or cost category; the CUR remains the line-item source. Remember the 11-09 note: allocation tags must be activated, then appear within about a day from activation onward.
Commitment telemetry: utilization vs coverage
Savings Plans utilization = the percentage of your committed $ per hour actually used (example: a $10/hour, 1- or 3-year commitment). Utilization below 100% means unused commitment — you bought too big. Savings Plans coverage = the percentage of eligible usage cost covered by Savings Plans in the period — the remainder bills at on-demand. Both reports live under Savings Plans → Inventory on the plan details. Same lens exists for Reserved Instances via the default RI reports.
Cost Anomaly Detection in depth
Create a cost monitor: choose Managed by AWS with a dimension — AWS services, linked account, cost allocation tag, or cost category — tracked automatically (new accounts and values join as the org grows), or a custom monitor with your own scope and thresholds. Attach an alert subscription; alerts reach SNS topics, chat applications, EventBridge, and AWS User Notifications, or individual alerts via User Notifications. Scope rule: monitors and subscriptions are visible only to the creating account, and monitors over linked accounts, tags, and categories must be created in the management account. Amazon Q Developer can assist with root-cause investigation of flagged anomalies.
Remediation: rightsizing, idle cleanup, scheduling
Act on recommendations: resize underutilized instances (rightsizing), delete idle resources — unattached EBS volumes, idle NAT gateways — and schedule stoppable workloads with Instance Scheduler on AWS, a CloudFormation-deployed solution that starts and stops tagged EC2 and RDS across Regions and accounts and supports hibernation for stopped EC2. A workload needed 50 of 168 weekly hours saves up to 70%; 24/7 fleets need it not. Fault-tolerant burst work belongs on Spot (up to 90% off).
Remediation: storage tiering and gp3 conversion
Known access patterns → lifecycle rules (transition or expiry, including noncurrent version cleanup). Unknown or shifting patterns → S3 Intelligent-Tiering: automatic tiering by access, no retrieval charges, objects return to Frequent Access when accessed, optional archive tiers for asynchronous data, a small per-object monitoring and automation charge, and the < 128 KB objects billed at Frequent rates with no fee. EC2 storage: Elastic Volumes converts gp2 → gp3 (or bigger/faster) online; unspecified performance provisions at least the gp3 baseline (3,000 IOPS) or the source-equivalent, whichever is higher; four modifications per rolling 24 hours, none cancellable, none shrinking.
Remediation: network — gateway endpoints vs NAT
Private subnets reach S3 and DynamoDB either via a NAT device or directly via a gateway VPC endpoint. The endpoint does not use PrivateLink (unlike interface endpoints) and carries no additional charge; every gigabyte that detours through a NAT gateway instead pays the NAT per-hour plus data-processing rates. First-move tactic: route S3/DynamoDB and keep the NAT only where generic egress is required.
The commitment decision and governance
Steady state → Savings Plans or RIs (1- or 3-year terms; SPs commit dollars-per-hour across compute incl. Lambda and Fargate, RIs reserve capacity per attributes). Unpredictable → on-demand; interruptible → Spot. Then hold the line: Budgets for planned thresholds (with optional actions), anomaly detection for pattern breaks, and the recurring Inform–Optimize–Operate cycle. The exam maps each job to its tool — consolidated recommendations (Hub), rightsizing (Optimizer), deviations (AD), hard limits (Budgets), commitment health (utilization/coverage reports).

FinOps
A culture practice joining engineering, finance, and business teams to trade off speed, cost, and quality and maximize business value of cloud spend.
FinOps phases
Inform, Optimize, Operate — see the spend, act on the waste, keep score.

Optimize phase

Operate phase
Governance and cadence: budgets, anomaly alerts, benchmarks, KPIs, and a recurring review with all teams.
Variable cost model
Cloud bills move with usage — capacity buying shifts from fixed capital expense to variable, usage-driven cost judged on value.
Ownership principle
Every team owns its cloud spend — engineers, not only finance, are accountable for the cost of what they build.
FOCUS
The FinOps Open Cost and Usage Specification — a vendor-neutral standard schema for cost and usage data.
Unit economics
Cost per business outcome (per customer, transaction, model run) — value-based metrics beat raw aggregate spend.
Cost categories

AWS Compute Optimizer
Opt-in ML service analyzing resource specifications plus CloudWatch utilization to produce rightsizing and idle-resource recommendations.

Compute Optimizer flags unused cost — e.g. idle EBS volumes or idle NAT gateways — for deletion or downsizing.

Savings Plans utilization
The percentage of your committed dollar-per-hour you are actually using — 100% means nothing burns idle; below 100% means an unused commitment you bought too big.
Savings Plans coverage
The percentage of eligible usage cost covered by Savings Plans in the period; the uncovered remainder bills at on-demand.
SP commitment shape
A $ per hour commitment (e.g. $10/hour) over 1- or 3-year terms, applied to compute including EC2, Fargate, and Lambda usage.
Cost Anomaly Detection
ML monitors that flag spend deviations from learned historical patterns — no fixed thresholds needed for the managed mode.
AD managed monitors
Managed by AWS: auto-track AWS services, linked accounts, cost allocation tags, cost categories — new accounts and values join automatically.
AD custom monitors
You pick the scope and the anomaly thresholds the monitor uses.
AD alert channels
SNS topics, chat applications, EventBridge, AWS User Notifications — subscriptions decide who hears an anomaly.
AD scope rule
Monitors and subscriptions are visible only to the account that created them; linked-account/tag/category monitors live only in the management account.
Budgets vs anomaly detection
Budgets compare spend against fixed planned limits; anomaly detection machine-learns deviations from real patterns.
Intelligent-Tiering (when to use it)
Unknown or shifting access patterns — automatic tiering with no retrieval charges and a small per-object monitoring fee; accessed objects bounce back to Frequent Access.
IT 128 KB rule
Objects smaller than 128 KB are not auto-tiered — always billed at Frequent Access rates and exempt from the monitoring fee.
S3 lifecycle policies
Rule-driven transitions between storage classes (and expirations) for known access patterns — the classic, free-of-fee tactic.
Elastic Volumes
Online EBS modification — change type, size, or performance without detaching or restarting; no charge for the modification itself.
gp2 to gp3 conversion
Online. Without specifying performance, EBS provisions at least the gp3 baseline (3,000 IOPS) or the gp2-equivalent, whichever is higher.
EBS modification limits
Up to four modifications per rolling 24 hours; a request cannot be cancelled and size never decreases.
Gateway VPC endpoints
Private connectivity to Amazon S3 and DynamoDB without an internet gateway or NAT device; no additional charge; not PrivateLink-based.
NAT gateway cost trap
NAT bills per hour plus per-GB data processing — route S3/DynamoDB through a gateway endpoint instead of through NAT.
Instance Scheduler on AWS
A CloudFormation-deployed AWS solution that stops/starts tagged EC2 and RDS across Regions and accounts using AWS Lambda; supports hibernation.
Scheduler savings example
Workload needed 50 of 168 weekly hours — business-hours-only fleets save up to 70% on instance hours.

Quizzes

Pop quiz drills the identify/remediate flow; real-world picks map situations to the right surface; the cost-tool drill pairs each job with its tool — first try counts.

1. Finance asks for one list with every optimization opportunity across the org, each priced with estimated savings. Where?

Show answer
Cost Optimization Hub — it consolidates and prioritizes rightsizing, idle-resource, and purchase recommendations across accounts and Regions and quantifies estimated savings, discounting your existing commitments so you only buy what adds savings.

2. Which two Savings Plans reports answer “how much of my commitment am I using” versus “how much of my eligible usage is still on-demand”?

Show answer
Utilization report: percent of the committed dollar-per-hour actually used (below 100% = unused commitment). Coverage report: percent of eligible usage cost covered by Savings Plans (below 100% = the rest billed on-demand). Both sit under Savings Plans → Inventory.

3. The team’s spend on one service jumped 40% mid-month with no budget line breached. Which tool flags it, and how would alerts reach the on-call?

Show answer
Cost Anomaly Detection — a managed monitor on services (or the account) learns the pattern and flags deviations without a fixed threshold; attach an alert subscription routing to SNS, chat applications, EventBridge, or User Notifications.

4. 300 development servers idle nights and weekends — give the remediation, and say which discount does not apply.

Show answer
Instance Scheduler on AWS: tag the instances, deploy the solution, and stop/start on a schedule (EC2 and RDS, hibernation supported). Business-hours-only fleets save up to 70%. Spot is the wrong tool — dev servers need predictable availability, not interruption tolerance.

5. Your EBS fleet is legacy gp2 and over-provisioned on IOPS. Walk the remediation without downtime.

Show answer
Identify with Compute Optimizer/Optimization Hub recommendations, then convert gp2 → gp3 with Elastic Volumes — modify online: no detach, no restart, no modification fee; gp3 baseline (3,000 IOPS) or higher applies; four modifications per 24 hours, and afterwards you can neither shrink nor cancel.

6. A data lake has unknown, changing access patterns and leadership is retrieval-fee-averse. Which storage class, and what are the two caveats?

Show answer
S3 Intelligent-Tiering — automatic access-pattern tiering with no retrieval charges. Caveats: a small per-object monitoring and automation fee, and objects < 128 KB stay billed at Frequent Access rates without tiering.

1. Leadership wants a single prioritized, organization-wide optimization view with savings quantified against current commitments.

Show answer
Cost Optimization Hub — consolidated recommendations across accounts and Regions with estimated savings net of your commercial terms.

2. Spend on one member account is drifting upward with no threshold ever defined — you still want automated flags.

Show answer
Cost Anomaly Detection — a managed monitor on the linked-account dimension machine-learns the pattern and alerts on deviations; created in the management account.

3. A project team must keep total monthly spend under a hard line, alerting at 80% of the plan.

Show answer
AWS Budgets — fixed planned thresholds (with optional actions); anomaly detection is the wrong lens because it flags pattern changes, not planned limits.

4. A steady production workload runs 24/7 and also uses Lambda plus Fargate — pick the purchasing tactic.

Show answer
Compute Savings Plans: a dollar-per-hour commitment for 1 or 3 years covering EC2/Fargate/Lambda compute; steady state is exactly what they reward.

5. Private subnets must reach S3 and DynamoDB, and finance rejects NAT charges on that path.

Show answer
Add a gateway VPC endpoint — reliable S3/DynamoDB connectivity with no additional charge, no internet gateway, and no NAT device.

6. Compute Optimizer flags idle NAT gateways; only S3 and DynamoDB traffic ever left through them.

Show answer
Remediate: delete the idle NAT gateways per the recommendation and route private S3/DynamoDB traffic through a free gateway endpoint — the optimization pair in one story.
